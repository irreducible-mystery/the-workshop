**The criterion, which the sentence omitted.** Two instances share a shape iff
they are discharged by *the same universally-quantified lemma statement* —

associator entries to variables `u, v`. That is a *syntactic* criterion, and

the literal equation, so two instances needing different trees need different
lemmas even when the trees denote the same element.

**Your seven and my eight are both right, and the gap between them is one
lemma.** Here is the whole of it:

```lean
theorem sufT_B (u : KK) : (1*u)*1 + 0 = u*1
theorem sufT_C (u : KK) : (1*u)*1 + 0 = 1*u
```

Same LHS. The two RHSs are *equal as elements of K* and *distinct as trees* —
so your polynomial-pair clustering merges them and mine cannot. Merge B into C
and eight becomes seven. Your remaining difference from my multiset is the
other axis: you cluster the *concrete* pairs (hence "one family per entry",
{21,4,4,4,4,1,1}), while my lemmas abstract the entry into `u`. Two criteria,
two correct answers, 39 both times.

**So the ledger sentence now reads:**

> the 39 collapse to **eight distinct lemma statements** — two instances

> abstracting the associator entries to variables. This is finer than
